
CareOrbit Website Brief v4 07-21-2026
Prepared for the CareOrbit Website design 2026 project. This is the working page-by-page brief for the new CareOrbit website: site goals, audience, messaging hierarchy, architecture, design system, and a full spec for every page.
Changed in v4, incorporating Jeff Coburn’s review (7/21): problem headline locked to the “costliest” phrasing; announcement bar reworded to drop the newbie-sounding “Now live”; the hero panel renamed “Find your orbit solution” sitewide; a new “Low risk, by design” section on the Platform page, condensed from the current selling deck; issuing-method flexibility added to the orbit explainer; trial stat labels standardized to the current deck.
Markers used throughout: [CONFIRM] means a decision or permission Michael needs to sign off before the item goes public. [PLACEHOLDER] means content that doesn’t exist yet or isn’t verified; build the slot, fill it later. A full content pass happens before launch; this brief defines structure, messaging, and the rules the content must follow.

1. What the site is for
One job: a sales lead lands on the site, understands what CareOrbit offers in under 90 seconds, clicks into the page built for their service line, sees the specific care and financial outcomes an orbit can deliver for them, and books a brief call or requests more information.
The site is not trying to close the deal, explain pricing, or serve patients. It exists to create qualified first meetings.
Success metrics: booked intro calls, info sheet requests, and service line page visits per session. Everything on every page should be judged against “does this move a visitor toward booking a call?”
What the site should feel like: operational, credible, ROI-driven. A 10+ year company with real hospital deployments and real trial data, not a startup brochure. Short sentences, real numbers, no hype.
2. Messaging hierarchy
Two selling lines lead the site. Everything else supports them.
Line 1, the hero headline: “Engaged patients are your most valuable asset.” This is the main headline on the front page. It flips the usual framing: engagement isn’t a cost center or a satisfaction checkbox, it’s an asset with financial value. The hero subhead then explains how CareOrbit turns that asset into measurable outcomes. [CONFIRM] one word: the current selling deck uses “most powerful asset,” this brief has been using “most valuable.” Pick one and standardize across site and deck.
Line 2, the problem headline (locked, per Jeff’s review): “Your costliest moments happen when your patients aren’t with you.” “Costliest” is the feel-the-pain word; “most expensive” reads as ambiguous (expensive care can be revenue, depending on who’s reading). This headlines the problem section on Home and sets up the orbit as the answer.
The two lines work as a pair: Line 1 states the value thesis, Line 2 states the gap. The orbit is the bridge. Every service line page inherits this logic in specialty-specific form.
3. Who it is for
Primary visitors (the people who book the call): service line managers and department heads, patient engagement and patient education leaders, and value-based care executives at hospitals and health systems.
Secondary visitors (the people who bless the deal): CFOs looking for ROI, CMOs and CEOs worried about market share and consumerism, MSO leadership (CMO, Finance, HR), and VA/DOD quality-improvement leadership.
Design implication: every page must serve two reading modes at once. The skimmer (executive) needs a headline, one proof number, and a CTA visible without scrolling effort. The validator (clinical and ops staff) needs the depth blocks further down: how it deploys, what it demands of staff, what the data shows. Structure every page top-down from skim to depth.

5. Conversion strategy (applies to every page)
Primary CTA: “Book a 20-minute intro call.” Pill button. Appears three times per page maximum: nav, mid-page after the strongest proof block, and the closing CTA card. [CONFIRM] final label (“intro call” is recommended over “demo” because the sales motion is a conversation that maps goals to a blueprint, and “20 minutes” lowers the commitment bar).
Secondary CTA: “Get the [service line] info sheet.” Email-capture form that sends the relevant PDF and feeds the lead list. The existing Platform Infosheet and Executive Summary are the first assets; service line versions follow via the existing sell-sheet pipeline.
No gated demos, no pricing CTAs, no chatbots. Two actions sitewide. (The “Find your orbit solution” panel on Home is a router, not a third CTA; it ends in one of the two actions.)
6. Design system
Layout grammar
Applies sitewide:
Generous whitespace; large rounded cards (radius ~22-28px); pill buttons; big confident headlines with tight letter-spacing; soft shadows.
Section headers: large two-line H2 (second line in a lighter tone) paired with a right-aligned intro paragraph.
Illustrated SVG imagery and real product screenshots instead of stock photography.
Split hero: headline + subhead + two CTAs on the left, interactive panel on the right.
Full-width deep-color rounded CTA card to close every major page.
Deep-color multi-column footer.
Color
From the Total Orbit brand guide (7/5/2026):
Ink Navy #0F1D2E: deep surfaces (announcement bar, CTA card, footer), headlines. Navy carries trust and clinical weight.
Harbor Blue #1E3A5F / Trust Blue #2D5A87: secondary surfaces and accents.
Signal Coral #E3735C: CTAs, eyebrows, active states only. Coral marks the active signal; never use it for large surfaces.
Paper #FFFFFF / Sky Mist #F3F6FA / Cloud Blue #EAF1F8: page backgrounds, cards, section fills.
Blush Pink #F2B8C6: wellness warmth, sparingly (patient-facing moments, Women’s Health accents).
Type and logo
Fredoka for headlines, Inter for body and UI. CareOrbit lockup (orbit-heart mark) in the nav; Total Orbit lockup in the footer as the corporate entity. Never recolor the orbit or stretch the mark; clear space of the center dot’s diameter on all sides.
Copy voice
Plain language, operational, numbers-forward. No “revolutionary,” no “best-in-class,” no “seamlessly.” Model sentence for register: “ordered the same way as bloodwork inside Epic.”
7. Content rules (non-negotiable)
No invented anything. No invented metrics, quotes, testimonials, case studies, or client references anywhere on the site. Every number traces to the Siteman controlled trial, the Siteman engagement results, or a clearly labeled model.
Client names. Only actual clients may appear as clients: Washington University, Siteman Cancer Center, BJC HealthCare. [CONFIRM] naming and logo rights for a public marketing site. Prospects (Mercy, AdventHealth, Rezilient, VA, and others) never appear on the site in any form until they are signed clients with permission.
Quotes and testimonials. The testimonial slot ships empty until real, permissioned quotes exist. [PLACEHOLDER: testimonial section, pending real quotes]. One named clinician quote outweighs any number of anonymous ones.
Trial data attribution. The five trial stats are always labeled “controlled clinical trial at Siteman Cancer Center.” Don’t blend trial results and deployment footprint into one causal story. Stat labels standardized to the current selling deck: patient understanding +65%, recovery navigation improved for 9 in 10, patient calls to the office −53%, patient satisfaction +22%, re-admission rate −41%. Satisfaction is labeled “patient satisfaction” generically; [CONFIRM] whether the instrument was HCAHPS before ever naming it.
Models vs. results. The cardiac rehab enrollment lift (+20-40% YoY) is a result; the revenue math built on it is a model and is always labeled as one.
Design-target framing for orbits without proprietary data: “every orbit is built backwards from at least one measurable outcome, tracked from day one,” never implied results. No borrowed industry statistics without a named citation Michael approves.
Supported claims only: build speed is “within a quarter” sitewide (the deck’s “weeks, not months” applies to orbit assembly specifically and may be used in that context); no competitor naming; no pricing.

Page 2: Platform (How It Works)
Purpose: satisfy the validator. This is where clinical, ops, and IT visitors confirm CareOrbit is real, light to implement, and safe to bring to their committee.
Must communicate: provider-anchored infrastructure, not a consumer app or a static content library; light implementation; plays well with your EHR; measurable by design; low risk to say yes to.
Sections:
Hero. “Enterprise engagement infrastructure for the moments that cost systems the most.” One-paragraph platform story. (Echoes Home’s problem headline; intentional.)

Assess: embedded PROMs, eScreenings, and outcomes capture tied to care pathways, quality initiatives, and value-based programs. Data: enterprise dashboards that make engagement, adherence, and outcomes measurable, actionable, and defensible, including an Engagement Index for every patient (one at-a-glance score, broken into knowledge, feeling, and actions, flagging topics not yet engaged or past due) and one-click export of any dashboard view.

Limited risk. No risk, no lock-in, no disruptions to your offices. A simple proposal you can say yes or no to; start with one orbit, one outcome.
Light lift for your staff. No new FTEs. You share the content and brand assets you already have; we assemble the orbit; you review and launch. Most of the work is assembly, not creation.
Secure from the start. HIPAA-compliant, BAA-ready, minimal PHI, hosted on AWS. (Anchor-links to the full compliance block below.)
Built to match your system. White-labeled in your brand and voice, built on your content, ROI goals, and care standards. Works with your EHR and your workflow, whichever issuing method fits: EHR order trigger, patient list, QR code, text message, or on-site registration.
