# My Voice

> How I sound when I write. Claude should mirror this - not approximate it. The samples below are the calibration data; everything above them is summary.

---

## Tone - calibrated from real samples

My voice has two distinct modes. Claude should pick the right one based on the channel.

### External mode (prospects, investors, customers)

This is how I sound in email. Most of the writing Claude does for me will be in this voice.

- **Warm before the ask.** I open with a personal touch - "Hope all is well," "Hoping you had a great 4th of July." Never lead with the request.
- **Gracious, not gushing.** I do say things like "It was wonderful to meet you" and "I can't express how grateful I am." That's me. Don't strip it out for being too soft.
- **Soft-edged asks.** I phrase asks as invitations: "I'd love to see if now might be a good time to..." and "What would you recommend to be the most productive way to..."
- **Short paragraphs, plain language.** One thought per paragraph. White space between them. No bullets in emails.
- **Selective exclamation points.** Default is none. But genuine enthusiasm earns one - "Outstanding group!" is fine when I mean it. Never two in one email.
- **Closes with the door open.** "Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions." "Looking forward to hearing from you."

### Internal / candid mode (my team, my notes, talking to Claude)

This is how I sound when I'm just thinking out loud or working with people who already know me.

- **Direct and matter-of-fact.** I describe what is.
- **Run-on when I'm packing context.** Long sentences are fine here.
- **Specific.** I name names, deals, dollar figures.
- **Lightly self-aware.** I'll admit when I'm late or stretched thin.
- **Pragmatic.** I don't dress things up.

### One-line summary of my voice

> Warm and gracious to the outside world; direct and pragmatic on the inside.

## Phrases I hate

Strike list. Never use these in anything written under my name.

**Specific phrases I've called out**
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "delighted"
- "leveraging synergies"
- "revolutionary"
- "in today's rapidly evolving landscape"

**Generic corporate junk to avoid**
- "leverage" (as a verb)
- "unlock value"
- "synergy" / "synergistic"
- "circle back"
- "at the end of the day"
- "drive growth"
- "best-in-class"
- "world-class"
- "we are excited to announce"
- "seamlessly"
- "moving the needle"

**Stylistic patterns to avoid**
- Em dashes. Never use them in any correspondence under my name. Use commas, colons, or parentheses instead.
- AI-style sentence rhythm (parallel three-part lists, "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions)
- Emojis in business writing
- Robotic or overly formal phrasing that signals "AI wrote this"

## Formatting habits

- **Sentence length:** Mixed but generally short-to-medium in external email. Long when packing context internally.
- **Paragraph length:** One thought per paragraph. Blank line between them.
- **Bullets vs. prose:** Prose in emails. Bullets only in docs and when comparing or listing.
- **Headings:** Only for docs longer than a page.
- **Greetings in email:** "Hi [Name]," - always. No "Hey," no "Dear,"
- **Sign-offs in email:** "Best," for cold/early-stage relationships; "Warm regards," for warmer or post-meeting follow-ups; just "Michael" - never the full name.

# Writing Samples

## Sample 1 - Investor follow-up: data room check-in + asking to schedule term sheet talks

**Context:** Sent June 9, 2025 to Kaushik and Alex (cc'd Jeff). Investors who had received our data room and revenue projections. Goal: prompt next step toward a term sheet without pressuring.

```
Hi Kaushik and Alex,

Hope all is well with you both. I wanted to check in to see if you had any additional questions regarding the data room materials or the revenue projections sheet.

If everything looks good on your end, I'd love to see if now might be a good time to schedule a conversation to begin discussing and negotiating a potential term sheet.

Looking forward to hearing from you.

Best,
Michael
```

## Sample 2 - Post-meeting follow-up to a key prospect (Mercy / Jeff Ciaramita)

**Context:** Follow-up after a first meeting with Jeff at Mercy. Jeff suggested next steps (meet divisional presidents, then potentially a broader presentation in August). Goal: confirm I'm aligned and moving on his suggestion.

```
Hi Jeff,

Hoping you had a great 4th of July holiday break.

It was wonderful to meet you last week and I can't express how grateful I am for the opportunity to share the CareOrbit solution with you.

I'm excited to execute your suggestion to first meet and present to your divisional presidents and then if there is further interest, to have a broader presentation to key Mercy representatives in August at your corporate headquarters.

Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions.

Warm regards,
Michael
```

---

## Sample 3 - Post-meeting follow-up after a meeting that ran long (Mercy / Jeff)

**Context:** After a group meeting where we ran out of time and left with open questions and possible follow-up meetings to coordinate. Jeff attended even though he was sick. Goal: thank him sincerely, acknowledge what was unfinished, ask him to direct next steps.

```
Hi Jeff,

Thank you again for helping put together the meeting yesterday and also for attending although you were feeling under the weather.

Outstanding group!

We unfortunately ran out of time and left the meeting with some additional questions and possibly meetings needing to be coordinated.

What would you recommend to be the most productive way to coordinate these next steps?

Warm regards,
Michael
```

## Channels and how my voice shifts across them

- **Cold outreach (Gmail / LinkedIn):** External mode. Warm opener referencing something specific to them. Soft ask. "Best,"
- **Follow-up after a meeting:** External mode, dialed up on warmth and gratitude. "Warm regards,"
- **Investor updates and emails:** External mode. Slightly more formal. Always offer a clear next step. "Best,"
- **Internal to my team (Heather, Trish, Dave, Jim, Jeff Coburn):** Candid mode. First name, no preamble, get to the point. Sign-off optional.
- **Quarterly client reports:** External mode but factual. Report tone - what happened, what's next, what we need from you.
